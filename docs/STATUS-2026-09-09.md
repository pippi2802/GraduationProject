# Thesis Status — 2026-09-09

> Snapshot of progress on "Improving Time Predictability for Time Sensitive Applications
> in Azure Kubernetes Services". Written after the academic supervisor meeting to
> consolidate where each RQ stands and what the refined experimental model looks like.

## Thesis structure

Three RQs, answered in order (each builds on the last):

- **RQ1**: Where do KubeDeadline's scheduling guarantees break down when deployed on AKS?
- **RQ2**: How to assign KubeDeadline parameters (budget, deadline, period) to time-sensitive
  tasks when execution-time characteristics are non-deterministic?
- **RQ3**: Which Azure IaaS configuration/infrastructure choices most reduce execution-time
  variability under KubeDeadline, and how should they be selected?

RQ1 establishes where/why guarantees break; RQ3's infrastructure choices exist to reduce the
noise RQ1 characterizes; RQ2's parameter model needs RQ1's real execution-time distributions
(contention effects included) to be grounded rather than idealized.

## RQ1 — essentially complete

Controlled co-location study on `research-questions/RQ1_final`, sieve-of-Eratosthenes probe
(`workload.c`), driven through KubeDeadline's DRA-based reservation mechanism.

**Design as actually implemented:**

- **model1** — baseline, target alone, no competitor. Control arm.
- **model3** (4 arms: `sib-cfs`, `sib-res`, `phys-cfs`, `phys-res`) — 2×2 grid:
  - `PAIR_TYPE`: target on competitor's SMT sibling vs. a different physical core.
  - `COMPETITOR_TYPE`: competitor unreserved (CFS, taskset-pinned) vs. reserved (its own
    CBS reservation at fixed intensity `u=0.4`).
- **model4** — periodic vs. event-triggered activation, isolating release/dispatch-latency
  jitter from steady-state contention effects.
- **Utilization sweep**: `[0.1 ... 0.9, 0.94]`, 10 points, deliberately dense near the ~0.95
  admission ceiling where the formal guarantee is most likely to break.
- **Two scales**: `tight` (10ms period, sieve_n=8192, cache-resident) and `soft` (100ms
  period, sieve_n≈1M, deliberately past this hardware's L2 — genuine cache-miss cost). Scale
  choice is justified by cache-hierarchy behavior, not just by "µs vs ms vs s" — documented
  directly in `workload.c`'s header.
- **Replication**: each arm re-run across 4 rounds (`round1`-`round4`), same fixed cluster,
  different points in time — gives temporal replication of the noisy-neighbor effect, not
  host-reallocation replication (cluster is a fixed, manually-provisioned set of Azure VMs,
  not ephemeral CAPZ-provisioned nodes).
- **Calibration gate**: cells with computed CV above `CV_THRESHOLD` (default 0.05) are
  auto-skipped rather than silently recorded — guards against the known short-duration
  noise floor contaminating results.

**Findings** (from prior RQ1 work, being reconfirmed/extended in RQ1_final):

- Guarantees hold cleanly under physical-core separation.
- Guarantees break down specifically under SMT-sibling sharing — up to 98% deadline-miss
  rates, driven by ~50% execution-time inflation from hyperthread contention (not by the
  scheduler withholding promised CPU time).
- Two distinct failure modes: mean-shift vs. variance-driven.
- Tested the paper's own formal guarantees (α, Δ) directly, not just deadline-miss rate.
- The original KubeDeadline paper's validation hardware had no hyperthreading — this
  breakdown mode was structurally unobservable in their evaluation.

**Known open item**: competitor intensity is held fixed (u=0.4) across the whole sweep,
by deliberate design (isolates utilization as the single varying factor rather than
multiplying dimensions) — needs to be stated explicitly as a scoping decision in the
methodology chapter, not left implicit.

## RQ3 — evidence collected, synthesis not yet written

- Isolation/hardening work (core isolation, IRQ steering, frequency pinning, boot-time
  mitigations) measured with real before/after impact: tick rate and IRQ activity driven to
  near-zero on isolated cores, worst-case hardware latency down ~90%.
- Identified a genuine, irreducible noise floor at short-duration/low-utilization cells —
  not fixable by further isolation, inherent to measuring small durations against fixed
  system jitter. Statistically pinned dispatch latency/preemption/context-switching as its
  drivers; hypervisor steal-time ruled out entirely.
- PREEMPT_RT investigated as a candidate infrastructure choice, found non-viable on this
  platform, with the failure precisely root-caused (citable negative result).
- **Still needed**: pulling this into an actual ranked/prioritized RQ3 answer. Ranking
  method not yet decided — plan is effect-size-based (variance reduction per intervention)
  rather than a purely qualitative ranking.

## RQ2 — approach decided, not yet built

- Plan: empirical resampling — use RQ1's measured execution-time distributions per
  co-location scenario as resampling pools, driven through a Monte Carlo simulation of
  H-CBS's own postponement/replenishment mechanism, to predict deadline-miss/tardiness
  behavior for parameter combinations beyond what was directly measured.
- Per-scenario execution/response-time distributions (the model's required input) have been
  built.
- Simulation itself not started. Open design questions: validation protocol (train on a
  subset of RQ1 scenarios, predict held-out ones, compare predicted vs. measured), and
  whether plain i.i.d. resampling understates tail risk if contention is temporally bursty
  (block bootstrap may be needed instead).

## From the 2026-09-09 supervisor meeting

Supervisor feedback: project direction is fine, but RQs need to be operationalized into
concrete, explicit experiment models — for each experiment, state environmental factors,
workload model, metrics, and the expected/hypothesized behavior up front, rather than
describing results after the fact. Specific points raised:

- Explicit choice of time scale (µs/ms/s) for the tasks under study, and how that choice
  interacts with known noise-floor effects.
- An explicit interference model: type, intensity, and temporal pattern of the competing
  workload, not just "present/absent".
- The delay-vs-variability tradeoff: increasing reservation slack (budget/period headroom)
  increases mean response time but decreases variability — worth an explicit sub-study
  (sweep utilization/slack, plot mean/tail response time against CV/IQR).

Cross-check against the actual RQ1_final implementation (this session) shows most of this is
already addressed: baseline control arm, 2×2 interference/placement grid, dense utilization
sweep including near-ceiling points, and a scale choice justified by cache behavior. Gaps to
close explicitly in the writeup: competitor-intensity scoping decision, and confirming
whether model4's periodic-vs-event arm is what the supervisor meant by "time of the effects".
