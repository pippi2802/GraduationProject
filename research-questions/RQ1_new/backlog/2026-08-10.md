**matmul vs ptrchase: why the results differ, and why that's expected**

The two probes stress genuinely different, separately-designed hardware resources — this isn't incidental, it's the point of picking them:
- **matmul** (`matmul.c`): dense multiply-add loop, working set sized to stay in L1/L2. Keeps the ALU/FPU issue ports busy essentially every cycle — no stalls, no gaps.
- **ptrchase** (`matmul.c --kind ptrchase`): pointer-chase over a 128MB buffer (`buf_kb=131072`, far bigger than the LLC), shuffled into one random Hamiltonian cycle via Sattolo's algorithm so the access pattern is unpredictable (defeats the hardware prefetcher). The loop is just `pos = buf[pos]`, repeated — each hop depends on the previous one's result, so it's a serial chain of ~100-300 cycle DRAM round-trips with no useful work fillable in between. `C_cputime_us` still measures full occupancy of the logical CPU (the OS doesn't context-switch a thread out while it's stalled on a cache miss), so C is large despite the execution ports being almost entirely idle.

The competitor/interferer always runs the *same* kind as the target (`generate_yaml.py` injects `--kind {workload}` into the interferer/competitor doc too, not just the target's) — so the sibling arms are matmul-vs-matmul or ptrchase-vs-ptrchase, never cross-kind.

Consequence, confirmed in Section C's numbers: sibling-placement arms (model3, model3-w2) show matmul C inflated ~1.8-2.1x by the co-runner, ptrchase only ~1.0-1.1x. Two threads both saturating the execution ports every cycle (matmul-vs-matmul) collide constantly — each gets roughly half the issue slots, hence ~2x. Two threads that are each idle-on-a-cache-miss almost all the time (ptrchase-vs-ptrchase) rarely want the port at the same cycle, so there's very little to contend over — this is close to the textbook case SMT is good at (filling one thread's stalls with another's work). Physical-placement control arms (model3-w3, model3-w4) show ~1.0x for both kinds either way, as expected — no shared execution ports once the co-runner is on a different physical core.

Considered and rejected: prime factorization as a second workload. Naive trial division is dominated by integer division (ALU/execution-port bound, small working set, nothing forces it out of cache) — it would land in the *same* contention category as matmul, not test a different resource. ptrchase was specifically chosen (and is the standard technique in the field, same idea as `lat_mem_rd`/LMbench) because it isolates memory/LLC-bandwidth latency, a resource `matmul.c`'s own comment notes explicitly: "the resource neither Kubernetes nor the DRA driver partitions."

**model3 / model3-w2 "coin-flip" miss rates: justified, not excluded**

Initially read the non-monotonic miss-rate pattern in `model3_round1/2/4` (soft) and the merged `model3-w2_round2` (tight) as the same liveness/coin-flip bug found earlier this project (a co-runner that looks alive but isn't). Checked properly by computing `alpha = C_p50/Q` per cell instead of just eyeballing miss rate:

- `model3_round1` (soft): every cell with α>1 shows ~100% miss, every cell with α<1 shows near-0%, no exceptions, across all 10 U values. Same clean split in round2 and round4.
- `model3-w2_round2` (tight, the merged-in rerun): α climbs from 0.87 (U0.1) to 1.29+ from U0.4 onward, and miss rate saturates at exactly 100% for every α well above 1 — including staying at 100% as U keeps increasing (initially flagged this as suspicious, reasoning a real effect should "ease off" at higher U; that reasoning was wrong — once α is well past 1, throttling hits nearly every job regardless of exactly how far over, so saturation *is* the expected shape, not a sign of a stuck/broken run).
- Spot-checked `placement.json` for cells on both sides of the α=1 line in both models — correct, consistent target/competitor placement every time. Not a placement bug either.

Verdict: the α≈1 sensitivity is a real, physically-explained H-CBS behavior (throttling is a hard cliff, not a gradual slope — the instant C exceeds Q, the job gets suspended until the next period and R roughly doubles), not measurement noise. **Decision: keep this data, report the α-crossing sensitivity as a finding rather than filtering it out.** matmul sits close to this threshold by calibration design and gets pushed over it by real sibling contention; that's exactly the mechanism the sibling-placement arms are meant to surface.

**Observation: why the flip shows up throughout `model3` (unreserved competitor) but only in one rerun of `model3-w2` (reserved competitor)**

Compared α across the two arms' clean rounds, tight scale:
```
model3 (unreserved), rounds 1+3:    α ranges 0.668 – 1.059 across U0.1–0.94, several cells crossing 1
model3-w2 (reserved), rounds 1+3+4: α ranges 0.720 – 0.874 across U0.1–0.94, comfortable margin throughout
```
`model3-w2` sits in a tight, safe band the whole sweep; `model3` sits chronically close to the edge and crosses it repeatedly. The mechanism: the *reserved* competitor has its own H-CBS reservation (`competitor.u = 0.3`), so it gets throttled too once it's used its own share of the period — it only actually contends with the target for roughly 30% of each period, then backs off. The *unreserved* competitor has no such cap; nothing else wants that sibling logical cpu, so it runs flat-out, continuously, for the entire period, every period. Bounded competitor → target pays for contention part of the time, stays under budget with margin. Unbounded competitor → target pays for it constantly, sits near the cliff, and ordinary jitter is enough to tip it over. Consistent with `model3-w2_round2`'s tight-scale flip being the one exception: that was a full rerun where α happened to land at 1.3–1.6, well outside the other three rounds' safe band, for reasons not yet identified (different calibration snapshot? different collection time?) — flips for the same reason `model3` does, once it's actually close to the edge.
